> **The short answer:** yes, €100 a month is enough to start. Build a small emergency fund first, then invest the same amount every month in a low-cost, diversified fund through a regulated platform, and leave it alone for years. Time does more of the heavy lifting than the amount.

"I'd love to invest, but I only have €100 a month. Is it even worth it?"

I hear this almost every week. In workshops, in coaching sessions, in messages after my TV segment. And every time, the person asking is a little embarrassed, as if investing were a club with a minimum entry fee.

It isn't. Not anymore.

## Firstly, is €100 a month really worth it?

Let me answer with numbers, because they surprise most people.

If you invest €100 every month and your investments grow by an average of 7% a year, this is roughly what you could end up with:

- After 10 years: about **€17,400**, from €12,000 put in.
- After 20 years: about **€52,400**, from €24,000 put in.
- After 30 years: about **€122,700**, from €36,000 put in.

Look at that last line again. Over 30 years, more than two thirds of the final amount is growth, not money you put in. That's compound growth: your returns start earning returns of their own.

*Illustration only. It assumes a steady 7% a year, which markets never deliver in a straight line, and ignores fees, tax and inflation. Returns can be lower, and you can lose money.*

So yes. €100 a month is not "too small". It's a beginning, and beginnings matter more than amounts.

## Step 1: make sure you have a safety net first

Before a single euro goes into the market, ask yourself one question. If my car broke down tomorrow, could I pay for it without touching my investments or a credit card?

If the answer is no, start there. Even a small emergency fund of one or two months of essential expenses changes everything. It means you will never be forced to sell your investments at the worst possible moment.

And if you carry expensive debt, like a credit card or a buy now, pay later plan, clear that first. No investment reliably beats 20% interest.

## Step 2: understand what you're buying

Most beginners in Malta start with ETFs. An ETF (exchange traded fund) is a basket that holds many companies at once. One ETF can give you a small slice of hundreds or even thousands of businesses around the world.

Two things make ETFs popular with beginners:

- **Diversification.** You're not betting on one company. If one struggles, it's one of hundreds.
- **Low fees.** Many index ETFs charge around 0.1% to 0.3% a year. Some actively managed funds charge 1% to 2.5% a year, plus entry fees.

Fees sound small. They're not. On €100 a month over 30 years, the difference between paying 0.2% and 1.5% a year could be around **€26,000** in my example above. Same money in, very different result.

Having said that, "an ETF" is not one thing. Some hold the 500 largest American companies. Others hold the whole world, bonds, or a single sector. Read the fund's factsheet before you buy. It tells you what it holds, where, and what it costs.

## Step 3: choose where to invest

You have two main routes in Malta:

- **A local bank or stockbroker.** Familiar, face to face, often higher fees.
- **An online investment platform.** Usually cheaper and easy to set up a monthly plan, but you do everything yourself.

Whichever you choose, check three things:

1. **Is it regulated?** Look it up on the MFSA register or on its home country regulator's website.
2. **What does it really cost?** Account fees, trading fees, currency conversion fees.
3. **Does it allow automatic monthly investing?** This is what makes €100 a month work.

How your returns are taxed in Malta can depend on the type of fund. Check this before you start, or ask a tax professional. It's a five-minute conversation that saves headaches later.

## Step 4: automate it, then leave it alone

Set up a monthly plan for the day after your salary arrives. Investing the same amount every month, whatever the market is doing, is called dollar-cost averaging. When prices are high, your €100 buys less. When prices fall, it buys more.

And then comes the hardest part of all: doing nothing.

Markets will drop. Some years they will drop a lot. The people who build wealth are rarely the ones who picked the best fund. They're the ones who kept going when it felt uncomfortable.

## What I tell everyone who starts small

I started small too. What changed my financial life wasn't a big amount. It was the decision to start, and then the habit of never stopping.

€100 a month won't make you rich next year. But in twenty years, you'll be very glad that today's version of you didn't wait for the "right amount".

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I really start investing with €100?

Yes. Many platforms let you invest small monthly amounts and buy fractions of funds. The key is low fees, so a small amount isn't eaten up by charges.

### What should I invest in first?

Most beginners start with a low-cost, diversified fund, such as a global or broad index ETF, rather than individual shares. Understand what the fund holds before you buy it.

### Is it safe to invest in Malta?

All investing carries risk, and the value of your investments can fall. Use a regulated provider, diversify, and only invest money you won't need for at least five years.

### Should I invest or keep saving in the bank?

Both. Keep your emergency fund in an easy-access account. Money you won't need for many years has historically grown more when invested, though there are no guarantees.
