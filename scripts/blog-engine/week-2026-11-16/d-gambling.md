She was gathering bank statements for a loan. She and her husband wanted to buy a second property to rent out.

Then she saw them. Several transactions to gambling companies. No less than €11,000.

The last time she'd seen anything like it was ten years earlier, when it had almost led them to financial ruin. She thought it was over.

When she asked him, he was quiet for a while. Then he said: "It's under control. It's only a small part of what I earn."

What she couldn't say out loud was her real fear. Ten years ago, when his own account wasn't enough, the joint account was next.

I answered her in my [Times of Malta column](https://timesofmalta.com/article/i-found-11000-bets-husband-bank-statement-now.1137025). This article goes deeper into what you can do, step by step, if you're in the same place.

## Firstly, protect what you can control this week

You can't control his gambling. You can control how exposed your family's money is. These steps aren't about punishment. They're about making sure one bad night can't undo years of work.

- **Keep the joint account to a working balance.** Only what's needed for groceries, bills and loan repayments. Nothing more sitting there.
- **Lower the daily limits** on the joint account and any shared cards. Your bank can help you do this quickly.
- **Move savings for your children** into an account in your name only, so they can't be moved without you.
- **Keep your own salary in your own account,** and transfer your share of household costs from there.
- **Turn on notifications** for every transaction on shared accounts, so nothing goes unseen.

None of this cuts him off. He still has his own income. It simply means the family's foundations are no longer within reach of the next bet.

## Pause the big decisions

This is the hard one. If you're about to take on a new loan, a second property or any big financial commitment, pause it.

A bank reviewing those statements will likely ask the same questions you're asking. And a rental property can wait a year. What you're trying to protect can't wait that long.

## The questions that tell you more than "it's under control"

"It's under control" is what almost everyone says. His actions will tell you more than his words. Ask him:

- Will you let me see your account from now on?
- Will you register on the Malta Gaming Authority's self-barring system?
- Will you install gambling-blocking software on your phone?
- Will you speak to someone who specialises in gambling problems?

If the answers are yes, that's a real start. If they're no, that's information too.

## Help exists in Malta, and it's for you as well

You don't have to carry this alone, and you don't need his permission to get support.

- **Supportline 179** is Malta's national helpline. Ask for Sedqa's gambling service. Sedqa supports families, even if the person gambling won't seek help.
- **The Responsible Gaming Foundation** can help with registering on the self-barring system.
- **The Malta Gaming Authority** runs self-exclusion and self-barring, and recommends blocking software for online gambling.
- **Caritas Malta** also runs support groups for people affected by gambling.

## What money steps can't fix

Before I close, one honest thing. You can protect your bank balance. You can't protect a marriage that way.

If he hides money from you, and you start hiding money from him, you end up with two people managing the same problem alone, in the same house. The practical steps buy you time and safety. They will never replace a real, adult conversation between the two of you, ideally with professional support in the room.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can my husband take money from our joint account without me knowing?

Usually, yes. Either holder can normally withdraw from a joint account. That's why keeping only a working balance there, with lower limits and notifications, matters.

### What is self-barring in Malta?

It's a system that blocks a person from gambling at land-based venues such as casinos and gaming parlours. For online gambling, players can self-exclude with licensed websites and use blocking software. The Malta Gaming Authority and the Responsible Gaming Foundation can help with the process.

### Where can families get help with gambling in Malta?

Call Supportline 179 and ask for Sedqa's gambling service, which supports family members too. Caritas Malta and the Responsible Gaming Foundation also offer support.

### Should we still apply for a loan?

It's wise to pause any new borrowing until things are stable and you both have full visibility of the household finances.
