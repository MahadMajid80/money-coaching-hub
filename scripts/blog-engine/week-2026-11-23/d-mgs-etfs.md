> **The short answer:** Malta Government Stocks (MGS) suit money you want to keep steady and earning a fixed income, especially if you'll hold them until they mature. ETFs that hold shares suit money you can leave for many years and can watch go up and down. Many people use both: MGS for stability and income, ETFs for long-term growth.

A 78-year-old reader wrote to me about an inheritance of €100,000.

The dilemma: buy two garages and rent them out? Or put the money into bonds paying 5.5% to 6% interest? The money wasn't needed for daily living, there were no big expenses coming up, and some ups and downs were fine.

Then came the detail that mattered most. Of an existing €444,000 portfolio, 64% was already in bonds.

The question was really one that many people in Malta ask: should my money sit in something safe that pays me interest, or in something that can grow?

## Firstly, what are Malta Government Stocks?

Despite the name, MGS aren't shares. They're bonds: a loan you make to the Government of Malta.

Here's how they work:

- You lend money, in units of €100, for a set number of years.
- In return, you get a fixed interest rate (the "coupon"), paid twice a year.
- When the stock matures, you get your €100 per unit back.
- They're listed on the Malta Stock Exchange, so you can sell before maturity. But then you get the market price on the day, which can be more or less than you paid.

The Treasury plans three to four MGS issues in 2026. Retail investors apply through Malta Stock Exchange members and other licensed investment firms, at a fixed price announced in advance.

## What do they pay right now?

The most recent issues give a feel for current rates:

- **August 2026:** 3.80% MGS 2036 and 4.25% MGS 2046.
- **October 2026:** 4.30% MGS 2037 and 4.50% MGS 2041.

Both were offered to the public at €100 per €100, so the yield, if held to maturity, was close to the coupon. Rates change with every issue, and each one's terms are announced one to two weeks before it opens. Check the Treasury's website for the next one.

In practice, if you put **€10,000** into the 4.30% MGS 2037:

- You'd receive **€430** a year in interest, paid as **€215** every six months.
- Interest on investments in Malta usually has 15% tax taken off at source for residents, so that would be about **€365.50** a year after tax.
- Over the roughly 11 years to 2037, that's about **€4,000** in interest after tax, plus your €10,000 back at maturity.

You know those numbers on day one. That's the big attraction.

## And ETFs?

An ETF (exchange traded fund) is a basket of many investments that you buy in one go. A world share ETF can hold thousands of companies across dozens of countries, usually for a low yearly fee.

There's no fixed interest and no maturity date. The value goes up and down every day. Over long periods, shares have historically grown more than bonds, but with some painful years along the way. Falls of 20% or more in a single year have happened several times, most recently in 2008. Over 10 years or longer, the ups and downs have tended to even out, but there's no guarantee.

## So which suits me?

Ask yourself three questions.

1. **When will I need this money?** Within a few years, or for a regular income, MGS held to maturity are a natural fit. If you won't touch it for ten years or more, ETFs give it room to grow.
2. **How would I feel if it dropped 20% in a year?** If the honest answer is "I couldn't sleep", lean towards MGS. Ups and downs only hurt when you sell in panic.
3. **What do I already own?** This is the one people forget.

That last question was the key for my reader. With 64% already in bonds, adding even more would mean being even more concentrated in one kind of investment. So I suggested looking at a two-part approach: part of the inheritance in quality bonds, to keep the regular income, and part in a diversified, low-cost share fund for growth, matching the reader's wish to hold more shares.

Think of MGS as the foundations and ETFs as the extension. Most homes need both.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Malta Government Stocks safe?

They're issued by the Government of Malta and pay a fixed interest rate, which makes them among the more stable investments for Maltese savers. But no investment is risk-free. If you sell before maturity, the price can be lower than you paid, and inflation can eat into fixed interest.

### How do I buy Malta Government Stocks?

When a new issue opens, apply through a Malta Stock Exchange member or another licensed investment firm, using the application form published by the Treasury. You can also buy existing MGS on the Malta Stock Exchange through a stockbroker.

### What is the minimum investment in MGS?

MGS are issued in units of €100. In recent issues, individuals could apply for up to €499,900 per stock. Your broker may set its own minimum or charge fees, so ask before you apply.

### Is it better to invest in bonds or ETFs?

It depends on your timeline, your income needs and how you handle ups and downs. Many people hold both. Bond ETFs also exist, holding many bonds in one fund.
